# Marc Cunningham Question Bank And Red Lines

## Five Primary Questions

### 1. What has changed most in your world since the old Broadspire disability build days?

Why ask it:

- it is personal to your shared history
- it invites Marc to compare then vs now
- it opens the door to people, process, market, and buyer-behavior changes

### 2. Where do you see the most friction now between the sales side and the teams that have to carry the work forward?

Why ask it:

- gets straight into handoffs
- lets Marc define the pain in his own language
- creates a natural opening for your operator-builder value

### 3. When opportunities get serious, where does executive time get wasted the most?

Why ask it:

- focuses on pain that a sales leader actually feels
- surfaces review chaos, unclear ownership, bad information flow, or pricing churn

### 4. How much of the proposal and content muscle today is truly reusable versus still being rebuilt under pressure?

Why ask it:

- connects directly to your Broadspire library work and later content-system experience
- surfaces whether the issue is tooling, governance, or behavior

### 5. If you could clean up one qualification, pricing, or proposal bottleneck tomorrow, which one would move the needle fastest?

Why ask it:

- pulls the conversation toward what matters now
- makes it easier to suggest a practical follow-up session instead of a vague "stay in touch"

## Follow-Up Prompts If The Conversation Deepens

- "Is that more of a process problem, a people problem, or a timing problem?"
- "Where does good information usually get lost?"
- "Does the issue start before proposal even gets involved?"
- "Is pricing helping the motion early enough, or only once things are already compressed?"
- "What tends to break first when volume spikes?"

## Listening Cues And What To Connect Back To

If Marc says:

- `handoffs are messy`
  - connect to your proposal governance and cross-functional coordination background
- `we keep reinventing content`
  - connect to the `1,600+` Broadspire library entries and your repository/process mindset
- `pricing gets pulled in too late`
  - connect to your Boss Key thinking around qualification, PTW discipline, and earlier operating checkpoints
- `executives get dragged into fire drills`
  - connect to your experience creating review structure and decision discipline
- `we have smart people but not enough operating consistency`
  - connect to The Facilities Group and your broader operator-builder framing

## Bridge Statements You Can Use In The Moment

### Bridge 1: from catch-up into value

"That is part of why I wanted to reconnect. The work I do now is still rooted in proposal and growth support, but it has broadened into how that whole operating chain works."

### Bridge 2: from his pain into your relevance

"That pattern sounds really familiar to me. A lot of my career has been translating exactly that kind of cross-functional friction into something more structured and repeatable."

### Bridge 3: from interest into a next step

"If it would actually be useful, I would enjoy a second conversation just focused on that operating layer. That has become a big part of how I think now."

## Red Lines

Do not:

- pitch public-sector facilities services as if that is the point of the call
- describe Boss Key like a mature software platform or an autonomous AI system
- make the conversation feel like a resume recital
- bury your Aetna and Broadspire relevance under newer experiences
- ask directly whether he is hiring unless he opens that door
- speak as if you already know Crawford's internal pain points

## Better Replacements

Instead of:

- "I built a company that does public-sector capture and proposal support."

Say:

- "I have been using Boss Key to formalize how I think about the operating chain around growth and proposals."

Instead of:

- "I think I could help Crawford with X."

Say:

- "I would be curious whether those same kinds of friction points show up in your world now."

Instead of:

- "I am looking for my next role."

Say:

- "I am open to the right problem and the right conversation."

## Final Reminder

The call does not need to end with an opportunity.

If Marc leaves with this impression, the conversation worked:

"Timothy grew from a strong proposal contributor into someone who understands how revenue, proposal, process, and execution fit together, and he still talks about the work in a grounded way."
