# Marc Cunningham Talk Track

## Tone To Hold

Warm, direct, and lightly reflective.

You are not trying to impress him with a pitch. You are helping him see the real arc from 2016 to now.

## Opener

"Marc, it is really good to talk to you. I was laughing when I looked back at our old messages because that Broadspire conversation ended up shaping a huge part of the next ten years for me."

If he asks how you have been before anything else:

"I've been good. Busy in a good way. The short version is that I kept following the same thread you first pulled me into, and it turned into a much bigger operating and proposal career than I would have guessed at the time."

## 60-90 Second Catch-Up Story

"When you recruited me into Broadspire, I was still very early. What I did not understand at the time was that I was getting pulled into exactly the kind of work I was wired for: taking messy growth situations and helping turn them into something structured and usable.

Since then I stayed close to that same problem from a few different angles. I worked in insurance and healthcare proposal environments, spent time in federal and commercial work, built content systems, supported large and complex pursuits, and eventually moved into proposal leadership roles where the job was not just writing but creating discipline across sales, finance, operations, and executive review.

What I am doing now through Boss Key is basically the next version of that same instinct. I am thinking less about proposal production as a standalone event and more about the full operating chain around it: qualification, capture thinking, pricing judgment, handoffs, content reuse, and execution discipline."

## Proof Points You Can Drop Naturally

Use only when relevant:

- "At SBM I produced more than 200 compliant proposals."
- "Back at Broadspire I built more than 1,600 proposal library entries, so even early on I was thinking about reuse and systems."
- "At The Facilities Group I was designing proposal operating models across multiple brands, not just managing individual responses."
- "I have stayed close to regulated and complex environments the whole time, including Aetna, Broadspire, federal work, and large service businesses."

## If He Asks, "What Are You Doing Now?"

"Right now I am building Boss Key as a consulting platform around operating improvement for growth and proposal work. The cleanest way to describe it is that I help teams tighten the chain between opportunity, qualification, capture thinking, pricing support, and proposal execution.

It is not a generic writing service, and I am careful not to overstate it as some AI platform. It is really an operating-model approach. A lot of the work is about reducing friction, making handoffs cleaner, and turning reactive proposal work into something more repeatable and less exhausting for the business."

## If He Asks, "How Does That Relate To Us?"

"The reason I think it relates is that the underlying problems usually rhyme. Different markets, same friction points: sales teams chasing growth, operational people carrying real expertise, proposal work getting compressed, executives getting pulled into chaos, and too much knowledge living in people's heads.

What I have gotten better at is helping that whole motion become more deliberate. Better qualification. Better content reuse. Better translation between sales, operations, pricing, and proposal. Better handoff from opportunity energy into actual execution."

## If He Asks, "Are You Looking For Something?"

"I am open to the right conversation more than I am chasing one specific format. I am not trying to force myself into a box. What interests me most is where there is a real operating problem to solve and where my mix of proposal, process, and cross-functional experience would actually be useful."

Shorter version:

"I am open, but I am trying to stay thoughtful about fit. The pattern I am most interested in is helping a growth team get more disciplined without creating more bureaucracy."

## Soft Close

"This has been great to hear. Honestly, one thing I would enjoy, if it is useful on your side, is a second conversation where we compare notes on where the friction is now in the growth-to-proposal chain. That has kind of become my lane over the last few years, and I would be curious what has changed in your world since the Broadspire build days."

## Fallback Close

"Even if nothing immediate comes out of it, I would love to know what kinds of operating problems feel hardest in your world now. It would help me understand where teams like yours are still getting jammed up."

## Conversation Map

Use this rhythm, not as a script but as a pacing guide:

1. Start relational.
2. Let the old Broadspire connection do some of the work.
3. Tell the short then-now-next story.
4. Ask thoughtful questions and listen for friction.
5. Only move toward a next step if the conversation earns it.
