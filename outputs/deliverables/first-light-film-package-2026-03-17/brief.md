# First Light Brief

## Core Idea
`First Light` is a 60-second proof-of-concept short film built as a lyrical personal-memory piece about wonder and childhood. It should feel intimate, light-led, and tactile. The film is not plot-driven. It moves like memory: fragments, sensory detail, implied family presence, and a final return to adult consciousness.

The emotional promise is simple: by the end of the film, the viewer should feel that memory begins in sensation before it becomes language.

## Story Spine
The short follows one emotional movement across a single morning-memory:

1. A child wakes into light before words.
2. The house reveals itself through curtains, dust, floorboards, water, leaves, and a nearby adult presence.
3. The memory opens outward into nature and a brief near-cosmic sense of scale.
4. The film returns to an adult perspective that recognizes this was the first grammar of home.

## Tone Pillars
- Reverent, not sentimental.
- Personal, but not diary-specific.
- Quietly cinematic, never theatrical.
- Observational and embodied rather than explanatory.
- Wonder first, nostalgia second.

## Visual North Star
Translate the user's stated reference into usable traits rather than imitation:

- Natural window light and backlight as the dominant visual engine.
- Drifting camera movement that feels handheld or softly floating, never locked-off and clinical.
- Childhood detail rendered through texture: fabric, wood grain, skin, water, leaves, dust, breath.
- Faces used sparingly; presence is often conveyed through hands, shoulders, silhouettes, reflections, and the back of the head.
- Occasional impressionistic inserts that widen the scale of the memory without turning the film abstract end to end.

## Visual System
### Color
- Warm gold
- Pale green
- Soft blue
- Gentle shadow with low-contrast blacks

### Light
- Early-morning sunlight through sheer curtains
- Window bloom and edge glow around subjects
- Airborne dust catching light
- Water reflecting soft highlights
- Exterior greens rendered softly, not hyper-saturated

### Camera
- Floating handheld or slow dolly feel
- Shallow depth of field
- Soft focus falloff
- Slow push-ins, side drifts, and observational pans
- No abrupt moves, whip pans, or aggressive stabilization

### Texture
- Cotton sleepwear
- Bare feet on floorboards
- Old wood, painted trim, ceramic bowl, sink water
- Thin curtains moving in a light breeze
- Leaves, grass, dew, and reflective water

## Character And World Continuity
### Child Memory Figure
- Age range: roughly 5 to 7
- Seen mostly in fragments, from behind, or in soft profile
- Soft brown hair
- Off-white or pale cream cotton sleepwear
- Bare feet

### Adult Presence
- Seen as hands, shoulders, doorway silhouette, or soft reflection
- Neutral earth-tone clothing
- Gentle, unhurried movement
- Presence should read as protective and ordinary, not dramatic

### Home
- Timeless domestic interior
- Wood floors, sheer curtains, simple sink, modest kitchen or hallway
- Leafy exterior visible through at least one window
- No visible branded products or technology

## Motifs To Repeat
- Light on dust
- Hands entering or leaving light
- Breath or fabric moving with air
- Water catching the window
- Leaves beyond glass
- A nearby person suggested more than shown
- Interior shelter contrasted with outside sky or water

## Production Guardrails
### Do
- Keep the film grounded in believable domestic reality even when it becomes impressionistic.
- Let the voiceover support the images, not explain them.
- Favor fewer, stronger images over coverage.
- Keep faces partial if character consistency begins to break.
- Maintain a continuous sense of one morning and one remembered world.

### Do Not Do
- Do not imitate a named filmmaker in prompts or packaging.
- Do not include hard sci-fi, surreal horror, magical creatures, or explicit fantasy events.
- Do not use lip-sync dialogue scenes.
- Do not show phones, tablets, televisions, logos, or modern branded objects.
- Do not over-style the home into luxury design imagery.
- Do not push color contrast into glossy commercial or fashion-film territory.
- Do not turn the adult presence into a melodramatic reveal.
- Do not use perfect smiling family tableau shots; the memory should feel observed, not staged.

## Narrative Constraints
- Runtime target: 58 seconds of picture plus 2 seconds of tail black.
- Format: 16:9 landscape.
- Voiceover target: 70 to 95 words.
- Shot count target: 9 shots.
- No dialogue exchange.
- No plot twist.
- Ending must return to adult awareness without breaking the sensory tone.

## Success Criteria
- The short reads clearly on first viewing without needing exposition.
- The emotional arc lands as wonder becoming memory.
- The film feels coherent even if full-face shots are removed.
- The visuals feel like one household, one child, and one light system.
- The piece remains strong both with audio and when watched muted for image rhythm alone.
