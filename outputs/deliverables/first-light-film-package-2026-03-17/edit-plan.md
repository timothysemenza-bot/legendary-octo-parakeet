# First Light Edit Plan

## Deliverable
Create one primary master cut of `First Light` at `1920x1080` in `16:9` landscape with a target runtime of `60 seconds`.

Recommended master specs:

- `1920x1080`
- `23.976` or `24 fps`
- `48 kHz` audio
- Stereo mix
- One clean master export plus one review export with burned-in timecode if needed

## Voiceover Script
Target length: `81 words`

```text
Before I knew the names for anything, I knew the light.
It came through the curtains and turned dust into weather.
It found the floorboards, my hands, the bowl in the sink, the leaves outside the window.
Someone moved in the next room, humming, and I learned the house was alive before I learned the word for home.
I think that is where memory begins: not in language, but in glow, water, breath, and the feeling of being held inside the morning.
```

## Edit Rhythm
- Let Shot 1 arrive gently. Do not begin with a hard cut from silence to image.
- Use straight cuts for most transitions.
- One or two dissolves are acceptable between Shots 5 to 8 if the memory wants to open up.
- Avoid montage choppiness. Every shot should feel long enough to breathe.
- Save the longest held emotional line for Shot 9.
- End on stillness, not a musical swell.

## Sound Design
### Voice
- Record close and dry.
- Speak softly, as if remembering rather than performing.
- Keep breath natural; do not over-edit every inhale.
- Favor one intimate take over a polished but emotionally distant read.

### Room Tone And Effects
- Light curtain rustle
- Distant floorboard or house creak
- Soft ceramic and water movement
- Leaves in light wind
- Almost-imperceptible room hum beneath the voice

### Music
- Optional, sparse, and supportive.
- Use a low piano, soft bowed texture, or warm ambient bed that enters gradually.
- No trailer pulses, no obvious rise-and-hit structure, no sentimental piano arpeggio cliche.
- If the score competes with breath or texture, remove it.

## Recording Workflow
### Scratch Take
1. Record a simple scratch voiceover first using the final script.
2. Aim for `56-62` seconds on the first pass.
3. Adjust line pauses until the rhythm matches the runtime map above.
4. Lock picture pacing to the scratch take before capturing the final read.

### Final Take
1. Record after the rough cut is assembled.
2. Match the scratch timing closely so visual timing does not collapse.
3. Capture `3-5` takes with small variations in pause length, not wildly different interpretations.
4. Choose the take with the most interior calm, not the cleanest announcer energy.

## Clip Generation Workflow
1. Generate or source candidate clips for Shots 1, 3, 6, and 9 first.
2. Build a rough cut using those anchors plus temporary stills for the remaining slots.
3. Replace temporary shots only after tone and timing feel correct.
4. If a shot keeps breaking continuity, convert it to a still-based motion shot instead of forcing another bad clip.
5. Keep each shot to one simple motion idea; do not stack multiple camera effects.

## Picture Lock Rules
- Keep the total runtime between `55` and `65` seconds.
- Preserve the shot order unless a generated clip clearly suggests a stronger neighboring swap.
- Do not add new plot beats or explanatory inserts.
- If the cut feels too literal, shorten or remove the most descriptive image rather than adding more narration.
- If the cut feels too abstract, strengthen Shots 4 and 6 before rewriting the voiceover.

## Acceptance Checklist
- Runtime lands between `55` and `65` seconds.
- Voiceover remains intelligible without sounding performed.
- The child, house, and light feel like one continuous memory world.
- The adult return lands clearly without needing a face reveal.
- The film works with and without music.
- The final shot echoes the earlier hand-in-light image strongly enough to close the loop.
