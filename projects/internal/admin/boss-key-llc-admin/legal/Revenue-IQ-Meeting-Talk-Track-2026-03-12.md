# Revenue-IQ Meeting Talk Track

## Opening

Chris, I appreciate you putting a draft together quickly and sending it over ahead of the meeting.

I agree with the core goals:

- protect each side's existing IP;
- keep client relationships clear;
- make sure AI/data use is controlled and professional; and
- set this up in a way that supports selling and delivery, not just theory.

My feedback is really about making the paper fit the working relationship we actually want.

## Framing

I want something that feels like a real partnership framework, not a one-sided lockup. If we get the structure right now, it will make client-facing work and any pilot rollout much easier later.

## Main points to make

### 1. Each side should keep its own pre-existing IP

I am fully aligned that Revenue-IQ should keep its methodology, commercial materials, and client-facing know-how.

I need the same clarity on the Boss Key side for existing tooling, workflow logic, prompts, architecture, reusable proposal operations, and anything else that is part of my standing platform and delivery method.

### 2. Client-specific deliverables can belong to the commercial vehicle

I am not trying to hold up ownership of the actual paid-for output. If we create a client-specific proposal, report, or draft artifact under a signed scope and it is paid for, that can belong where we agree it should belong.

What I want to avoid is language that also transfers the underlying engine, workflow, or reusable tooling used to create it.

### 3. The AI clause should be precise

I agree that neither side should take the other side's confidential information and feed it into public or shared model training.

What I want to tighten is the current wording around fine-tuned models and deletion so it does not accidentally sweep in the platform layer, logs, backups, security records, or reusable technical assets.

### 4. The client-protection language should be narrower

I understand the intent, and I am not looking to go around anyone.

At the same time, a 24-month restriction, 25% recurring fee for two years, and 100% revenue damages provision is too heavy for me to sign. I would rather use a narrower, practical introduced-client non-solicit with a sensible referral structure if that situation ever comes up.

### 5. We should pair this with an SOW

The fastest way to make this real is to use a framework agreement for IP/confidentiality/relationship guardrails and put scope, deliverables, fees, acceptance, and pilot terms into an SOW.

That gives both of us cleaner paper and reduces misunderstandings later.

## Closing

If the direction of the counter-draft makes sense, I think we are close. My goal is not to slow anything down. It is to make sure the agreement protects both of us and gives us a workable base if we decide to move forward together.
