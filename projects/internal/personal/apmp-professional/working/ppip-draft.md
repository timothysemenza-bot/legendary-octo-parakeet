## STAR Content

### Situation
- The Facilities Group and RNA faced a Detroit Public Schools recompete from November 2025 through January 2026.
- The opportunity was strategically significant because the incumbent work represented more than `25%` of RNA's book of business, and RNA wanted to expand from about `$14M` of current scope to an `$85M` opportunity.
- There was no meaningful capture plan in place at release, and the senior leaders overseeing the opportunity had not fully read the `RFP`.
- The candidate, as Senior Manager of Proposals, was still a team of one on many concurrent bids and had an external consultant added midstream without prior handoff planning.

### Tasks
- `Establish bid governance and schedule`
  - Impacted: all senior stakeholders plus the external consultant
  - Necessary because no usable capture plan or agreed review cadence existed at release
- `Develop evaluator and win-theme strategy`
  - Impacted: RNA executive team, consultant, and presentation speakers
  - Necessary because the team needed a customer-centered strategy, not an internally driven narrative
- `Elicit and structure SME input`
  - Impacted: pricing, operations, engineering, Cheryl, and other contributors
  - Necessary because the proposal could not be credible without turning live operational knowledge into compliant content
- `Draft and quality-control proposal and presentation content`
  - Impacted: consultant, executives, final reviewers, and evaluators
  - Necessary because the written response and finalist presentations had to stay aligned and submission-ready
- `Lead review and submission readiness`
  - Impacted: executive reviewers, consultant, and whoever might need to submit in the candidate's absence
  - Necessary because readiness could not be left to last-minute judgment

### Activities
- `Establish bid governance and schedule`
  - Activities: generate milestone plan in Power Automate, verify it with leadership, issue project plan, keep meetings and milestones visible
  - Responsible/accountable: candidate responsible; executive leadership informed and aligned
  - Timing: began immediately at `RFP` receipt; recurring cadence throughout the pursuit
- `Develop evaluator and win-theme strategy`
  - Activities: research evaluation-board members, prepare customer-focused strategy before kickoff, use Cheryl's insight to refine positioning
  - Responsible/accountable: candidate responsible; executives validated direction
  - Timing: one-time start with recurring refinement as new facts emerged
- `Elicit and structure SME input`
  - Activities: interviews, calls, emails, Teams follow-ups, compliance-matrix updates, pricing/staffing mediation, Cheryl solutioning sessions
  - Responsible/accountable: candidate responsible for coordination; SMEs accountable for factual input
  - Timing: recurring from kickoff through final review
- `Draft and quality-control proposal and presentation content`
  - Activities: AI-assisted first drafts from live inputs, manual refinement, Word read-aloud QA, consultant enablement, speaker notes, question-prep notes
  - Responsible/accountable: candidate led about `60%` of drafting and all coordination; consultant handled about `40%` of drafting and structural content
  - Timing: recurring across the full `20`-working-day window and finalist-presentation period
- `Lead review and submission readiness`
  - Activities: create SharePoint structure, align executive review, decide when content was submission-ready, prepare handoff for candidate absence, support `2` finalist presentations
  - Responsible/accountable: candidate responsible and effectively accountable for readiness discipline
  - Timing: one-time setup early, then recurring checks through submission and finalist rounds

## Effort And Operating Cadence
- Candidate handled `100%` of coordination and about `60%` of drafting.
- Chris Arlin handled about `40%` of drafting and no coordination.
- Executives and SMEs primarily contributed through live calls, reviews, and factual inputs rather than independent drafting.
- Active stakeholder set: about `15` people whose inputs and decisions had to be reconciled.
- Meeting cadence: frequent working sessions, at peak as much as twice daily with senior leadership; additional one-on-one and small-group sessions with Cheryl, pricing, operations, engineering, and the consultant.
- Monitoring tools: Power Automate schedule, compliance matrix, SharePoint structure, recurring verbal assignments backed by email/Teams follow-up.
- AI and automation role: supportive tools only; they accelerated planning, compliance extraction, and draft production inside a candidate-led process.

## Results And Measurement

### Current Defensible Results
- Compliant Detroit Public Schools submission completed inside about `20` working days.
- The pursuit advanced to `2` finalist presentations.
- About `12` later RNA school bids reused Detroit-developed content, graphics, formatting, bios, or narrative structure within about `1` month of the candidate's departure.
- In the immediate aftermath, about `6` school bids were active at once and used the Detroit baseline.
- The candidate's time to reach a first compliant draft dropped from about `4` hours of work per school bid to an about `80%` draft in roughly `5` minutes when the Detroit baseline fit the opportunity.
- Later school bids typically required only one executive review focused on operations and technical solution rather than repeated writing and structure review.
- Bid-specific effort shifted toward about `3` hours of pricing and solutioning meetings plus light administrative completion of forms and signatures rather than rebuilding the narrative base each time.
- Executive confidence and trust in the proposal function improved because the Detroit work showed how dependent the organization was on disciplined proposal leadership.
- Executive review friction decreased because later bids reused a more familiar school-bid structure instead of repeatedly introducing unfamiliar formats.

### Before / After Framing
- `Before:` ad hoc, frantic, low-trust school-bid process with inconsistent structure
- `After:` more templated and reusable school-bid baseline for RNA
- `Before:` first compliant school-bid draft required about `4` hours of fresh drafting effort
- `After:` a reusable Detroit baseline allowed an about `80%` draft in roughly `5` minutes before compliance tailoring
- `Before:` executives did not fully understand proposal workload or governance needs
- `After:` greater scrutiny, seriousness, and respect for proposal-management discipline
- `Before:` each school bid felt structurally different to leadership
- `After:` familiar sections and formatting lowered review burden on later bids, often reducing review to one executive pass

### Measurement Caveats
- Final award result is unknown.
- Later turnaround improvement is based on candidate recollection from live production work rather than retained time logs.
- Ann can corroborate rapid draft turnaround, continued customization for compliance, and the pricing team's focused review of bid-specific changes, but she was not the final approver of every draft.
- Artifact access is indirect, so measurements should stay tied to candidate recollection plus what Ann can verify.
