## STAR Story 1
- Title: Tampa finalist-presentation war room
- Best-fit competency: Meeting leadership
- Situation: While at the company summit in Tampa, the team received finalist-presentation notice at about `3:00 PM` with less than `48` hours to respond.
- Task: Calm senior leaders, interpret the request quickly, and set a workable preparation strategy on the spot.
- Action: Candidate built a rapid PowerPoint summary without being asked, convened the room, mapped the requirements to proposal themes already developed, set clear next actions, and turned a potentially chaotic evening into a focused one-hour planning session.
- Result: The team aligned quickly, started work that evening, and delivered the finalist presentation on time; the bid ultimately reached `2` finalist presentations.
- Lessons learned: In a high-pressure room, structured facilitation and visible priorities matter more than trying to solve everything verbally.

## STAR Story 2
- Title: Pricing and staffing mediation that kept Detroit on schedule
- Best-fit competency: Decision making and delegating responsibility
- Situation: Pricing rationale and staffing assumptions were creating friction between Ann Couzins and RNA leadership during the Detroit bid.
- Task: Clarify what the `RFP` required, keep the pricing workbook aligned with the narrative, and stop the dispute from consuming the schedule.
- Action: Candidate mediated the calls, translated what each side needed from the other, drafted narrative options, clarified a confusing pricing section of the `RFP`, and pushed the team toward agreed assumptions and next steps.
- Result: Pricing assumptions were settled, staffing was aligned, the Excel rationale structure moved forward, and the bid stayed on schedule instead of stalling on one disputed topic.
- Lessons learned: When experts talk past each other, the proposal lead has to translate intent, not just record comments.

## STAR Story 4
- Title: Building subcontractor routing intelligence for the Bank of America pursuit
- Best-fit competency: Innovation
- Situation: The Facilities Group did not have a strong way to show reliable subcontractor coverage across a large multi-site facilities opportunity for Bank of America.
- Task: Create a more precise, convincing way to demonstrate subcontractor proximity and coverage for a roughly `$200M` pursuit.
- Action: Candidate learned the haversine formula approach, combined available vendor data with a Google Cloud scraping solution, mapped subcontractor locations, and shared the approach with vendor management, sales leadership, and IT.
- Result: The pursuit used the most complete subcontractor coverage data the team had assembled; the technique was reused on later bids, and IT put a similar capability on its roadmap even though the pursuit itself was not won.
- Lessons learned: Innovation matters most when it turns a vague answer into a concrete, evaluator-friendly proof point.
