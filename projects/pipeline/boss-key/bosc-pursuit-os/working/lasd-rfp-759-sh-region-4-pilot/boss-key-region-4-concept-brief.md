## Working Title

`Region 4 under one operating rhythm`

## What LASD Should Believe Fast

- Region 4 will be managed as one operating network, not twenty-eight unrelated janitorial stops.
- Public lobbies, restrooms, and high-touch zones will stay inspection-ready without losing control of secured areas.
- Supply continuity will be managed actively because Region 4 depends on contractor-furnished inventory.
- Labor, payroll, and healthcare compliance are built into the staffing model, not patched in later.
- Emergency calls will have a real response path with hard timing behind it.

## 90-Second Brief

Region 4 is not hard because it is dirty. It is hard because it is scattered, public-facing, and unforgiving when small misses repeat across many sites. LASD needs one contractor that can hold a dispersed station portfolio to one standard for cleanliness, replenishment, and response without creating labor-compliance problems or callback churn.

The Boss Key story for this bid is disciplined operating control. Region 4 requires contractor-furnished supplies, mixed staffing windows, public-lobby responsibility, and reliable follow-through when a site issue or emergency request appears. A credible proposal has to show how inspections, replenishment, staffing oversight, payroll controls, and emergency escalation all work together across the full footprint.

If we keep this as the live pilot, the proposal should make LASD feel that Region 4 will be calmer to manage: fewer supply misses, faster corrective action, cleaner public-facing areas, and less administrative risk around wage, healthcare, and staffing commitments.

## Core Themes

### One control rhythm across the region

Use one inspection and deficiency-close process across all sites, with clustered supervision and visible escalation.

### Visible quality where the public notices first

Lead with public lobbies, restrooms, touchpoints, and complaint-prone areas rather than generic floor-care language.

### Inventory discipline for Region 4

Show how one-to-two-week supply continuity is preserved and how shortages are prevented before they become a visible service failure.

### Compliance that protects service stability

Connect living wage, payroll controls, ACA Gold coverage, and staffing retention to dependable daily execution.

### Emergency response with hard commitments

Anchor the story in the 30-minute callback rule, the four-hour estimate requirement, and a named after-hours response path.

## Likely Evaluator Objections

### Objection: this reads like office cleaning, not sheriff facilities

Counter:
Use the language of secured areas, background clearance, public lobbies, callback work, and PRS-backed quality control.

### Objection: low cost will hollow out the staffing model

Counter:
Show how healthcare, payroll compliance, and staffing oversight are already carried inside the operating plan.

### Objection: twenty-eight facilities will feel loosely managed

Counter:
Show one regional operating cadence with clustered oversight and issue-close discipline.

### Objection: contractor-furnished supplies will create outages

Counter:
Lead with inventory thresholds, replenishment cadence, and shortage contingencies.
