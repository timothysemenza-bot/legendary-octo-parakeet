You are not trying to sound like a content creator.
You are trying to sound like the useful person a buyer would trust on a call.

Target tone:
- direct
- calm
- specific
- field-tested

Avoid:
- fake intensity
- broad motivational language
- generic business jargon

## Clip structure

Use this template for most videos.

1. Hook
- one sharp sentence

2. Problem
- name the operational issue

3. Explanation
- give 2 to 4 concrete points

4. Close
- one CTA only

Example:
- hook:
  `Most facilities proposals do not break in the writing.`
- problem:
  `They break in the handoff between pursuit logic, pricing, staffing, and compliance.`
- explanation:
  `That is why the draft feels rushed even when the team is working hard.`
- close:
  `Comment "handoff" if you want the checklist.`

## Batch-recording workflow

Run this once per week.

1. Pick 4 ideas from the 30-day calendar.
2. Write one hook and 3 bullets for each.
3. Record all 4 in one 45-minute block.
4. Choose the best 2 for LinkedIn that week.
5. Save the other 2 for the following week.

## Editing rules

Keep editing light:
- trim dead air at the start and end
- add burned-in captions
- add a simple title at the first frame if useful

Do not add:
- aggressive zooms
- meme cuts
- heavy music
- flashy transitions

## Caption rules

Because LinkedIn supports captions and video controls, use captioned uploads as the default.

Guidelines:
- keep text large and centered within safe margins
- use short lines
- make the first caption line match the hook
- do not let captions sit under interface elements

## Thumbnail rules

If you choose a thumbnail:
- use a frame with direct eye contact
- keep headline short
- use 3 to 6 words max

Examples:
- `Why Good Firms Lose`
- `The Owner Bottleneck`
- `Timing Beats Volume`
