# Boss Key Video Recording Runbook
Date: 2026-03-13
Owner: Boss Key LLC

## Goal

Create a repeatable talking-head system that makes Timmy look current, useful, and credible without turning the content into fake influencer theater.

## Platform reality

The current LinkedIn environment rewards:
- identifiable experts
- native video
- captions
- consistent topical series

That means the fastest authority path is:
- Timmy on camera
- one sharp point per clip
- native LinkedIn upload first

## Minimum gear

Use:
- iPhone or modern smartphone
- tripod
- cheap lav mic or wired earbuds if the room echo is bad
- window light or one soft key light

Do not wait for:
- pro camera
- studio
- complex B-roll

## Visual standard

Frame:
- chest-up shot
- camera at eye level
- clean background
- slight depth behind you if possible

Look:
- jacket, overshirt, or clean structured top
- no distracting branding unless it is Boss Key
- neutral background with one strong visual element max

## Recording setup

1. Face the light source.
2. Put the camera slightly above eye line.
3. Keep the phone vertical if you want easy Shorts/Reels reuse.
4. Record 3 takes max.
5. Stop if the clip feels over-rehearsed.

## Delivery style

You are not trying to sound like a content creator.
You are trying to sound like the useful person a buyer would trust on a call.

Target tone:
- direct
- calm
- specific
- field-tested

Avoid:
- fake intensity
- broad motivational language
- generic business jargon

## Clip structure

Use this template for most videos.

1. Hook
- one sharp sentence

2. Problem
- name the operational issue

3. Explanation
- give 2 to 4 concrete points

4. Close
- one CTA only

Example:
- hook:
  `Most facilities proposals do not break in the writing.`
- problem:
  `They break in the handoff between pursuit logic, pricing, staffing, and compliance.`
- explanation:
  `That is why the draft feels rushed even when the team is working hard.`
- close:
  `Comment "handoff" if you want the checklist.`

## Batch-recording workflow

Run this once per week.

1. Pick 4 ideas from the 30-day calendar.
2. Write one hook and 3 bullets for each.
3. Record all 4 in one 45-minute block.
4. Choose the best 2 for LinkedIn that week.
5. Save the other 2 for the following week.

## Editing rules

Keep editing light:
- trim dead air at the start and end
- add burned-in captions
- add a simple title at the first frame if useful

Do not add:
- aggressive zooms
- meme cuts
- heavy music
- flashy transitions

## Caption rules

Because LinkedIn supports captions and video controls, use captioned uploads as the default.

Guidelines:
- keep text large and centered within safe margins
- use short lines
- make the first caption line match the hook
- do not let captions sit under interface elements

## Thumbnail rules

If you choose a thumbnail:
- use a frame with direct eye contact
- keep headline short
- use 3 to 6 words max

Examples:
- `Why Good Firms Lose`
- `The Owner Bottleneck`
- `Timing Beats Volume`

## CTA library

Use only these for now:
- `Comment "diagnostic" and I will send it.`
- `DM me "radar" if you want the brief.`
- `Comment "handoff" and I will send the checklist.`
- `If this is your situation, message me and I will tell you what I would fix first.`

## Filming prompts

Use these when you need a quick script.

1. `Why good janitorial firms still lose bids`
2. `The owner should not be the proposal manager`
3. `Three signs the rebid is already visible`
4. `The four-part handoff that breaks most proposals`
5. `Why waiting for the RFP is too late`
6. `What 200+ compliant proposals taught me`
7. `Timing beats volume in facilities pursuits`
8. `What I look for in a 20-minute briefing`
9. `Why proposal chaos usually starts upstream`
10. `The difference between proposal writing and pursuit discipline`

## Recording checklist

Before recording:
- phone cleaned
- mic tested
- notes reduced to bullets
- one CTA selected

Before posting:
- captions checked
- opening 2 seconds are strong
- CTA matches the asset you will actually send
- title and description are consistent with the rest of the campaign

## Repurposing workflow

Each good video becomes:
- LinkedIn native post
- Short/Reel
- transcript turned into a text post
- one quote line for a graphic or document asset

## Performance review

Judge videos by:
- comments
- profile views
- DMs triggered
- call conversations created

Do not obsess over raw views if the right people are responding.
