# Boss Key Comment and DM Funnel
Date: 2026-03-13
Owner: Boss Key LLC

## Objective

Turn content attention into:
- useful conversations
- asset requests
- briefing calls
- first paid work

Do not jump from post impression to pitch.
Move people one step at a time.

## Funnel stages

1. Public signal
- someone comments
- someone likes multiple posts
- someone views the profile
- you comment intelligently on their content

2. Warm interaction
- connection request
- asset send
- one useful follow-up question

3. Diagnostic conversation
- short DM exchange
- 20-minute facilities opportunity briefing

4. Paid first step
- Opportunity Foresight Sprint
- Facilities Pursuit Diagnostic Sprint
- live capture or proposal support

## Who to engage

Priority accounts:
- owner-founders of janitorial and facilities-service firms
- proposal managers and capture leads in facilities or public-sector service businesses
- operations leaders who influence pursuit execution
- APMP-adjacent professionals who may refer work

Do not spend most engagement time on:
- generic creator accounts
- people who only talk about hustle and mindset
- broad sales influencers with no market overlap

## Comment strategy

Leave comments that do one of three things:

1. Diagnose

Example:
`The operational issue here is that most teams do not spot the pursuit bottleneck until pricing and compliance are already colliding late.`

2. Reframe

Example:
`I would frame this less as a writing problem and more as an upstream qualification and handoff problem.`

3. Add field reality

Example:
`This is especially true in facilities work where the owner is often still carrying too much of the bid process personally.`

## Comment rules

- be specific
- be useful
- avoid generic praise
- do not force an offer into every comment
- stay in lanes where you have real experience

Bad comment:

`Great post. So true.`

Better comment:

`The part I see most often is the handoff failure between ops assumptions and proposal structure. By the time the writing team feels the problem, it is already late.`

## Connection request templates

For facilities owner:

`I work around facilities pursuit discipline and proposal operations. Thought it made sense to connect.`

For proposal leader:

`I spend a lot of time in proposal and pursuit process work for facilities-service firms. Would be glad to connect.`

For referrer or adjacent expert:

`Your work overlaps with public-sector growth and proposal operations. Thought it would be useful to connect.`

## DM workflow after someone comments

### If they comment `diagnostic`

Message 1:

`Thanks for commenting. Here is the Facilities Pursuit Diagnostic.`

`If you want, after you look at it, tell me which area feels weakest right now: visibility, qualification, handoff, or review cadence.`

Message 2 after they reply:

`That makes sense. In firms your size, that usually affects more than one part of the process. If helpful, I can tell you the first thing I would tighten before the next pursuit.`

Message 3 if there is real pain:

`If you want to work through it live, we can do a short facilities opportunity briefing and I can map where I would start.`

### If they comment `radar`

Message 1:

`Thanks. Here is the latest Mid-Atlantic Facilities Rebid Radar.`

`Out of curiosity, which geography or account type matters most for you right now?`

Message 2:

`That helps. I am tracking this series because most firms see posted bids too late. If you want, I can point out the signals I would watch first in that lane.`

### If they comment `handoff`

Message 1:

`Thanks. Here is the handoff checklist.`

`Which break shows up most often on your team now: pricing, staffing, compliance, or review cadence?`

### If they simply react but do not comment

Message:

`Thanks for engaging with the post. I spend a lot of time on facilities pursuit discipline and proposal operations. If that is relevant on your side, happy to compare notes.`

## DM rules

- ask one question at a time
- keep each message short
- do not send paragraphs
- do not force the call too early
- only move to a briefing when there is visible pain, timing, or curiosity

## When to suggest a briefing

Suggest `Book a facilities opportunity briefing` when:
- they describe an active pursuit or recurring bid problem
- they ask what to do next
- they show urgency around an account or rebid
- they have consumed at least one asset and responded thoughtfully

Do not suggest a briefing when:
- they are only being polite
- they are outside the target lane
- they have not signaled any operational pain

## Follow-up cadence

After asset send:
- follow up in 2 business days if they replied to the first question
- follow up in 5 to 7 business days if they went quiet after showing real interest
- stop after 2 follow-ups unless they re-engage

## CRM logging

Track every meaningful interaction in the creator scoreboard:
- source post
- trigger keyword
- account type
- follow-up status
- call booked or not

## Language to avoid

Do not say:
- `just bumping this`
- `circling back`
- `would love to pick your brain`
- `let me know if you need proposal writing`

Use:
- `I can tell you what I would check first`
- `Here is the asset`
- `Which part feels weakest right now?`
- `If useful, we can work through it live`
