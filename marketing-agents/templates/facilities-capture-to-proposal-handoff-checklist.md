# Facilities Capture-to-Proposal Handoff Checklist
Date: 2026-03-13
Owner: Boss Key LLC

## Purpose

Use this before writing starts.

The goal is to make sure capture logic, pricing, staffing, compliance, and review cadence are aligned early enough that the proposal team is not forced to reconstruct the pursuit under deadline pressure.

## 1. Opportunity logic is explicit

Confirm:
- why this opportunity fits
- why now
- what the likely buying problem is
- what the evaluator is most likely to care about
- what the must-win themes are

Red flag:
- the team is pursuing because the bid exists, not because the fit is clear

## 2. Bid-no-bid decision is actually made

Confirm:
- named decision owner
- documented go/no-go call
- known competitor or incumbent context if available
- known gaps or vulnerabilities
- reason the team believes it can win

Red flag:
- nobody can explain why this should be a real priority

## 3. Staffing assumptions are stable enough

Confirm:
- who will deliver the work
- any site leadership assumptions
- labor model assumptions
- where staffing is still uncertain
- whether staffing claims match the actual operating model

Red flag:
- the narrative is being drafted around staffing claims that are still moving

## 4. Pricing and narrative are on the same track

Confirm:
- pricing owner is named
- pricing timeline is clear
- proposal lead knows what pricing assumptions drive the story
- pricing changes will be communicated fast
- the team knows what can and cannot be promised at the current price point

Red flag:
- pricing is building in isolation while content is already taking shape

## 5. Compliance structure exists before drafting

Confirm:
- compliance matrix exists
- requirements are assigned
- missing information is visible
- page limits, attachments, forms, and certifications are mapped
- submission format and logistics are known

Red flag:
- compliance review is expected to happen after drafting is mostly done

## 6. SMEs know what they owe and when

Confirm:
- each input owner is named
- each owner has a deadline
- prompts are specific, not vague
- proposal lead has a follow-up plan
- review expectations are known

Red flag:
- the team is still saying `we need input from ops` without defining exactly what that means

## 7. Review cadence is scheduled now

Confirm:
- pink, red, or equivalent review dates are set if needed
- executive review expectations are clear
- final quality control owner is named
- approval chain is known
- production deadline is earlier than the actual submission deadline

Red flag:
- the only real review event is the night before submission

## 8. Storyline is grounded in operations

Confirm:
- differentiators reflect real delivery capability
- the narrative is tied to staffing and service model reality
- proof points are supportable
- the proposal is not overclaiming transformation language
- the team can defend the story in an interview or follow-up

Red flag:
- the proposal sounds polished but disconnected from how the work will actually be performed

## Fast scoring

Score each section:
- `2` = clear and controlled
- `1` = partially clear, still moving
- `0` = weak or missing

Interpretation:
- `13-16` = likely ready for execution with light support
- `9-12` = needs tighter coordination before drafting accelerates
- `5-8` = high risk of downstream chaos
- `0-4` = stop and rebuild the pursuit structure first

## Follow-up CTA

Use this when sharing the checklist:

`If you want, send me the section that feels weakest and I will tell you what I would tighten first.`
