# Boss Key Modern Authority Campaign
Date: 2026-03-13
Owner: Boss Key LLC

## Campaign thesis

Boss Key should be marketed as a person-led authority business, not as a brochure site or a generic proposal service.

The market needs to experience Timmy as:
- the operator who understands why good facilities contractors still lose bids
- the proposal and capture advisor who can explain complex pursuit problems in plain language
- the identifiable expert behind the offer, not a faceless firm

The website supports credibility.
LinkedIn drives attention, familiarity, trust, and inbound conversations.

## Core positioning

Primary positioning line:

`I help janitorial and facilities-service firms turn field capability into cleaner, earlier, more winnable public-sector pursuits without forcing the owner to run the whole bid process alone.`

Secondary positioning line:

`I help public-sector growth teams get earlier visibility, tighter pursuit discipline, and a cleaner handoff from capture logic to proposal execution.`

What the market should remember:
- you have 12+ years in proposal management
- you built 200+ compliant proposals at SBM Management Services
- you led facilities-services proposal operating-model work at The Facilities Group
- you are not selling hype, software, or generic bid writing
- you are selling judgment, discipline, and credibility

## Campaign objective

90-day outcome:
- build Timmy into a recognized facilities/public-sector pursuit voice on LinkedIn
- create recurring inbound from comments, DMs, referrals, and profile views
- convert that attention into diagnostics, briefings, and paid first-step work

Success targets:
- 3 to 5 qualified briefing calls per month
- 2 to 3 content-triggered DMs per week
- 1 recurring monthly flagship content asset
- 1 to 2 paid first-step engagements within 90 days

## Audience

Primary audience:
- owner-led janitorial and facilities-service firms

Secondary audience:
- proposal managers, capture leads, and business-development leaders inside facilities firms

The first credibility lane is facilities.

## Modern platform rules

1. Build around an identifiable expert, not a company logo.
- LinkedIn's own thought-leadership guidance emphasizes identifiable authors and subject matter experts over faceless brand content.

2. Use native video regularly.
- LinkedIn currently supports native video posts, captions, thumbnails, and visibility/comment controls.
- Keep the phone-video habit simple and repeatable.

3. Do not make every post a booking-link post.
- Most posts should create engagement or DM interest first.
- Use comments, DMs, and featured profile assets to move to the next step.

4. Make the content useful before it is promotional.
- Strong thought leadership should challenge assumptions, provide usable insight, and show a real point of view.

5. Use one newsletter, not multiple.
- LinkedIn supports newsletters, but the campaign should start with one recurring series only:
  - `Mid-Atlantic Facilities Rebid Radar`

6. Use document and PDF assets to look serious.
- Consultant trust often rises when a post points to a diagnostic, scorecard, radar brief, or memo that feels concrete.

7. Be willing to show your face.
- In today's environment, Timmy is part of the product.
- Talking-head video is not optional if the goal is authority at speed.

## Measurement

Track:
- posts published
- video posts published
- comments made on other people's content
- DMs started
- asset requests
- calls booked
- paid work opened

Do not optimize only for vanity:
- views matter

## Platform notes used for this campaign

Current LinkedIn facts confirmed on 2026-03-13:
- LinkedIn supports native video uploads, captions, thumbnails, and visibility/comment controls.
- LinkedIn video uploads currently allow up to 15 minutes and require safe margins because UI can overlap edges.
- LinkedIn newsletters can notify subscribers by push, in-app, and email.
- LinkedIn supports up to five newsletters, but the most active/recent newsletter gets the most profile visibility.

Implication:
- start with one newsletter
- post native video consistently
- make Timmy the visible author
- use PDFs and newsletter editions as credibility multipliers, not as the main publishing habit
