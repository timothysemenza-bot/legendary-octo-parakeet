# New Albany Elementary Parking / Pick-up / Drop-off Briefing (New Albany-Plain Local Schools, Ohio)

## 300–500 word summary
The relevant campus appears to be **New Albany-Plain Local Schools (NAPLS) in New Albany, Ohio**, where elementary-grade operations are split between **New Albany Primary School (grades 1–3)** and **New Albany Intermediate School (grades 4–6),** both on the district’s shared learning campus. The district’s campus page describes a 200-acre consolidated campus and links dedicated parent pick-up/drop-off procedures for both buildings, plus a grade 1–12 traffic map. This confirms that the “New Albany Elementary” context is best mapped to these two schools rather than a single school formally named “New Albany Elementary.”

Official guidance is prescriptive and flow-oriented. Both Primary and Intermediate pages require drivers to remain in vehicles, load/unload only on the curb side, and keep loops continuously moving. Both also state that loop traffic should not stop longer than necessary and should remain single-file to reduce crash risk. The procedures explicitly prohibit parking in active loops and direct drivers needing to enter buildings to designated lots. At the Primary school, one loop (McCoy loop) is one-way with a required right-turn exit to Dublin-Granville Road; exiting against traffic is explicitly prohibited. Primary guidance also assigns specific loops by hallway/color and states families with multiple children should pick up at the youngest child’s location.

The district’s transportation page adds system context: route data is released in PowerSchool, route changes are expected during enrollment adjustments, and families should anticipate 2–4 weeks of route stabilization at school-year start. While this concerns bus operations rather than parent-car loops, it indicates a dynamic transportation environment that can affect campus traffic patterns.

On documented concerns: the district materials do not publish quantified congestion statistics (queue lengths, incident counts, citation volumes), but the rules themselves repeatedly emphasize safety and traffic-control risk points (continuous flow, one lane, curbside loading, no parking in loops, crosswalk caution). That framing indicates congestion and conflict-risk management is an operational priority.

Public-comment infrastructure exists through Board of Education participation rules and BoardDocs meeting records. However, in this research pass, no board item or local media article was found that specifically documents a discrete New Albany Primary/Intermediate parking controversy or a recent policy dispute over parent vehicle management. Press references found were generally district transportation-related (e.g., broader bus staffing/operations) rather than elementary-loop parking policy.
