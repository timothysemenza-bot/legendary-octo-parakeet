  Executive Summary
  CurePSP has built meaningful advocacy momentum through leadership, volunteer
  commitment, community advocates, and people directly impacted by PSP, CBD, and MSA.
  This plan is designed to help carry that momentum into a more consistent federal policy
  rhythm while keeping CurePSP's people-first values visible in every step.
  The approach is practical for a one-person launch: focus on federal work first, align with
  current advocacy priorities, keep close coordination with the Steering Committee and
  leadership, and create clear public-facing progress updates so the broader CurePSP
  community can stay informed and engaged.

Why Pennsylvania Is A Practical State Pilot

  Pennsylvania is not included as a token state add-on. It is a strategic pilot based on proximity,
  infrastructure, and mission fit.


Reason 1: CurePSP Care Infrastructure Is Already Present
  The University of Pennsylvania Parkinson's Disease and Movement Disorders Center in
  Philadelphia is listed as a CurePSP Center of Care.
  Lehigh Valley Health Network's Fleming Memory Center in Allentown is also listed as a
  CurePSP Center of Care.
  This gives the pilot credible clinical anchors connected to people living with PSP, CBD, and MSA
  and their care partners.

Reason 2: State-Level Neurological Research Momentum Exists
  Pennsylvania's 2024-2025 budget included a $5 million allocation for neurodegenerative
  disease research.
  The administration described this investment as support for treatment and cure pathways tied
  to conditions such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.
  This creates a policy environment where CurePSP's mission can connect to active public
  investment conversations.

Reason 3: Operationally Feasible From Day One
  Pilot scope can remain narrow while federal work stays primary.
  Close regional access supports in-person relationship development where useful.
  Early pilot feedback can improve future state strategy without overextending resources.

Execution Standards For The First Six Months
1) Federal-First Discipline                       focus

    Keep federal advocacy as the core lane in year one.
    Use Pennsylvania only as a focused pilot that supports, not distracts from, federal progress.

2) People-Centered Policy Practice
    Integrate people with lived experience and care partners into planning and outreach.
    Ensure messaging reflects real patient and family realities, not abstract policy language.

3) Clear, Consistent Communication

    Provide concise updates that leadership, board members, and community members can all
    follow.
    Show what was done, what moved, what did not move yet, and what happens next.

4) Continuous Improvement Without Ego
    Welcome course correction quickly when tactics are not landing.
    Keep the focus on mission progress, not process defensiveness.


Immediate Next Steps If Selected
1. Confirm first 30-day priorities with leadership and Steering Committee.
2. Launch appropriations-focused outreach calendar and shared tracker.
3. Publish first monthly update for both internal teams and community audiences.
4. Start Pennsylvania pilot scoping tied to Centers of Care and existing state context.
