## Product brochure vs proposal argument assessment

### Currently reads as product/tech brochure
- Offer-led framing early in the page (“3 offers,” “tracks”) before fully defining buyer pain.
- Repeated automation wording in top-level copy, which pulls positioning toward tools/implementation rather than judgment and operating system design.
- “Results” and “pricing tracks” are generic categories without a clear proposal narrative (problem -> method -> risk reduction -> engagement decision).
- Top-level CTA language varies (“strategy call,” “fit call,” “open scheduling calendar”), weakening the decision path.
- Mini-game and high visual novelty elements compete with proposal seriousness.

### Proposal argument elements that already exist (but are under-leveraged)
- Founder credibility and years of experience are present.
- Engagement sequencing exists (“How we work”) but is fragmented.
- Trust center and policy reduce enterprise-risk concerns.

## Biggest conversion blockers

1. ICP and buyer situation are too broad.
- “Service businesses” is wide; pain language is not specific enough to trigger self-identification fast.

2. Too much implementation framing too early.
- Automation/process tooling is mentioned before clear business outcomes and executive-level operating problems.

3. Proof is weakly structured.
- Logos and background exist, but there are no concise testimonial slots, no case-story placeholders, and no explicit risk-reduction narrative.

4. Engagement path is unclear.
- Multiple CTA variants and no single diagnostic path create friction.

5. Narrative order is not proposal-native.
- Homepage sections are organized like service cards + features, not as a persuasive proposal flow.

6. Contact conversion lacks structured intake.
- No short diagnostic form to capture core buyer context (name, company, what they need fixed).
