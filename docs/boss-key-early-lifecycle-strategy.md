# Boss Key Early-Lifecycle Strategy

## Strategic summary

Boss Key should operate as a boutique **government opportunity intelligence and capture advisory firm** built around a two-partner model:

- Partner 1: proposal and capture strategy
- Partner 2: government policy and program intelligence

The firm’s core value chain is:

```text
Policy Intelligence
-> Opportunity Forecasting
-> Capture Strategy
-> Proposal Execution
```

Chosen launch defaults:
- Region: Mid-Atlantic
- First wedge: facilities services
- Revenue model: retainer + project expansion

The firm is not:
- a lobbying shop
- a generic proposal writing vendor
- a SaaS platform

The firm is:
- an early-lifecycle advisory partner for contractors, service providers, nonprofits, and selected public-sector vendors that need to act before procurements are formally released

## Business model

### Core thesis
Sell recurring intelligence and advisory visibility first, then expand into capture strategy and proposal execution as opportunities mature.

### Revenue mix
- monthly intelligence retainers
- fixed-fee capture strategy projects
- fixed-fee proposal execution work
- selective success-linked upside only in narrow cases

### Market statement
Boss Key helps public-sector contractors and mission-driven organizations identify and shape winnable government opportunities before formal procurements are released.

## Service architecture

### Lifecycle model
```text
1. Policy / Procurement Intelligence
   -> budgets
   -> agendas
   -> legislation
   -> program shifts
   -> regulatory movement

2. Opportunity Forecasting
   -> likely buying events
   -> target accounts
   -> service-line alignment
   -> timing hypotheses

3. Capture Strategy
   -> pursuit qualification
   -> stakeholder mapping
   -> positioning themes
   -> teaming decisions
   -> pre-RFP action plans

4. Proposal Execution
   -> proposal architecture
   -> compliance framing
   -> pricing support
   -> production / review support
   -> AI-assisted proposal operations
```

### Core offers

#### 1. Opportunity Foresight Sprint
- 3-4 weeks
- paid entry offer
- best for first engagement
- deliverables:
  - target-market signal scan
  - likely opportunity forecast
  - top-priority pursuit shortlist
  - capture brief for highest-priority targets
  - partner recommendation call

#### 2. Procurement Intelligence Retainer
- monthly or biweekly monitoring of target agencies, authorities, districts, and accounts
- deliverables:
  - signal digest
  - 90-180 day opportunity watchlist
  - agency priority summary
  - risk / confidence framing
  - recommended next actions

#### 3. Capture Strategy Engagement
- triggered once a likely procurement is identified
- deliverables:
  - opportunity brief
  - stakeholder map
  - competitor / incumbent assessment
  - positioning memo
  - pursuit action calendar

#### 4. Proposal Execution Support
- triggered at solicitation stage
- deliverables:
  - proposal architecture
  - compliance / outline structure
  - content planning
  - review support
  - pricing coordination
  - AI-assisted proposal ops

## Ideal customer profiles

### Primary ICPs

#### Regional facilities and municipal service contractors
- janitorial
- EVS
- day porter
- maintenance
- staffing / operational support
- roughly $5M-$75M revenue

Pain points:
- reactive bidding
- poor rebid visibility
- limited internal capture discipline

#### Mid-size service providers growing in state and local
- healthcare service providers
- workforce and human-service operators
- education support providers

Pain points:
- understand service delivery but not procurement timing
- understand bids but not policy/funding signals upstream

#### Nonprofits pursuing government-funded work
- education
- workforce
- community health
- human services

Pain points:
- can respond to grants and bids
- lack opportunity forecasting discipline before release

### Secondary ICPs
- public-sector technology vendors entering regional government markets
- specialty subs that want better visibility into prime-contractor opportunities

### Best-fit characteristics
- public-sector growth intent
- weak pre-RFP visibility
- no dedicated intelligence function
- no mature capture team
- willingness to act early

### Funding and budget signals
- proposed and adopted budgets
- capital plans
- bond programs
- appropriations
- pass-through funding
- agency budget hearings
- district and authority spending plans

### Legislative and regulatory signals
- bills and committee activity
- executive orders
- rulemaking
- licensing or compliance changes
- Medicaid / public health program changes
- education and municipal service mandates

### Procurement-adjacent signals
- contract expirations
- rebid windows
- amendments and change orders
- board approvals
- sole-source renewals
- procurement forecasts
- pre-solicitation notices
- vendor outreach events

### Political and governance signals
- council agendas
- board and commission agendas
- committee minutes
- public authority meetings
- audits
- complaints and performance controversies

### Program and operating signals
- new program launches
- pilot expansions
- staffing shortages
- facility openings / closures

- compliance failures
- emergency procurements
- service delivery changes

## Go-to-market plan

### First 12 months

#### Phase 1: establish the wedge
- lead with Mid-Atlantic facilities and public-service markets
- position around “see the opportunity before the bid”
- publish 3 flagship themes:
  - signals that predict rebids and outsourced service buys
  - how budgets turn into procurements
  - why contractors lose by waiting for the RFP

#### Phase 2: relationship-led pipeline
- start with existing facilities and government-contracting relationships
- target introductions from:
  - incumbents and subcontractors
  - APMP / proposal ecosystem contacts
  - consultants serving government contractors
  - accountants, brokers, staffing/service partners
- core sales motion:
  - founder-led outreach
  - warm introductions
  - educational briefing
  - paid foresight sprint
  - retainer conversion

#### Phase 3: authority and trust
- monthly signal brief
- small executive webinars
- 2-3 public mini-reports per quarter
- short case examples:
  - signal
  - likely opportunity
  - capture implication
  - client action

## Pricing and deal-partner model

### Pricing
- Opportunity Foresight Sprint: $7,500-$15,000
- Narrow agency/account sprint: $5,000-$7,500
- Light intelligence retainer: $3,000-$5,000/month
- Core intelligence retainer: $6,000-$10,000/month
- Deeper intelligence/capture retainer: $10,000-$18,000/month
- Opportunity-specific capture engagement: $8,000-$20,000
- Quarterly capture support: $15,000-$35,000
- Proposal execution support: $10,000-$75,000+

### Deal partners

#### Referral partner
- warm introduction only
- 10% of first contract value
- one-time payment

#### Selling / channel partner
- materially influences qualification and close
- 15%-20% of first contract value
- 3-6 months on retainers if active in the close

### Guardrails
- no indefinite overrides
- no commission stacking without approval
- no arrangement that blurs into lobbying or improper procurement influence
- Boss Key keeps scope, pricing, and delivery ownership

## Two-partner operating framework

### Partner 1: capture and proposal strategy
- lead offer packaging for capture and proposal services
- lead qualification, capture plans, pricing coordination, and proposal operations
- own downstream expansion from intelligence into execution

### Partner 2: policy intelligence
- own signal monitoring framework
- lead legislative, budget, and program interpretation
- produce policy-to-opportunity briefs, stakeholder maps, and agency priority analysis

### Shared responsibilities
- strategic business development
- weekly signal review
- weekly pipeline review
- go / no-go on clients and pursuits
- thought leadership

### Weekly cadence
- Monday: signal review and opportunity watchlist
- Wednesday: active client delivery review
- Friday: pipeline and next-week actions

### Minimum operating stack
- CRM: HubSpot or equivalent simple pipeline tracker
- research stack: government sources, agendas, procurement portals, Notion/spreadsheets
- delivery stack: signal brief, forecast memo, stakeholder map, capture brief, decision memo
- proposal stack: existing proposal and AI-assisted workflow under Partner 1

## First 90 days

### Days 1-30
- finalize positioning and narrative
- productize the Opportunity Foresight Sprint, intelligence retainer, and capture engagement
- build reusable deliverable templates
- define the initial Mid-Atlantic facilities signal universe
- build a target list of 50-75 prospects

### Days 31-60
- launch warm outreach
- publish first two thought-leadership assets
- run 5-10 discovery calls
- close 1-3 paid foresight sprints
- refine messaging based on objections and close patterns

### Days 61-90
- convert sprint clients into retainers
- identify 2-3 capture projects from intelligence work
- formalize 3-5 referral or channel partner relationships
- package one adjacent healthcare or education variation
- start a simple operating dashboard:
  - leads
  - discovery calls
  - paid sprints
  - retainer conversions
  - downstream capture / proposal revenue
  - average contract value
  - cycle time to first paid engagement
